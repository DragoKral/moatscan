This week's picks again focus on small, overlooked companies: under $3B in market value, followed by few Wall Street analysts, beaten down from their highs, and still profitable at their core. The idea is to be contrarian where it's justified. Small stocks swing harder and can be thinly traded, so keep positions small and use limit orders. Nobody can reliably predict which stocks rise in the next few weeks; these are the best odds over a year or more, not promises. Changes from last week: the same five names, but Sturm, Ruger moves up now that Beretta's buyout offer has closed. Travelzoo's estimated value is lower after a fix to how the scan counts cash that really belongs to customers. Cass Information Systems dropped off the cheap list for the same reason.

1. Innoviva (INVA) · ~$21, estimated value ~$49, if no growth ~$27

A $1.5B royalty-and-drugs company that only five analysts follow. Even if it never grows, the cash it earns is worth more than today's price.

Latest news: No big news since Q2, when revenue rose 19% to $120M and hospital drug sales rose 46%. The reported loss came from its stock holdings in other companies falling in value, mainly Armata Pharmaceuticals, not from the business. It holds about $570M in cash.

How they plan to grow: It uses steady royalties from GSK lung inhalers (about $60M a quarter) to build a hospital-drugs business, with at least $150M of US sales expected this year. It also has a newly approved pill for drug-resistant gonorrhea (Nuzolvence). Analysts expect about 9% revenue growth.

What to watch: Q3 results, expected around Nov 4, and early sales of the new pill. Risk: the royalties fade as the inhalers age, and swings in its investments keep making profits look messy.

2. Build-A-Bear Workshop (BBW) · ~$25, estimated value ~$34, if no growth ~$33

The classic contrarian setup. It's down about 65% from its high after a forecast cut, yet it still expects $60–68M of pre-tax profit this year on a $310M market value. Only three analysts cover it.

Latest news: In late August it cut its 2026 revenue forecast to $500–525M after Q2 sales fell 7% and online sales fell 16%. Since then, it kept its dividend and unveiled its biggest-ever store, a three-story flagship at ICON Park in Orlando.

How they plan to grow: More stores and partner-run locations abroad, wholesale deals that put its products in other retailers, and "experience" flagships like Orlando. It returned $23M to shareholders in the first half.

What to watch: The Orlando flagship opens in November, Q3 results come in early December, and then the holiday season makes or breaks the year. Risk: spending on kids stays weak and online sales keep falling.

3. Sturm, Ruger (RGR) · ~$43, estimated value ~$55, if no growth ~$54

A debt-free US gun maker with a long record of profits, followed by only two analysts. Its CEO and directors bought shares with their own money this year.

Latest news: This is a special situation. Italian gunmaker Beretta's offer to buy shares at $44.80 closed on Oct 15. So many shares were offered that Beretta can take only about 38% of each holder's shares, and it will end up owning about 25% of Ruger. The FTC has proposed rules limiting how Beretta can place people on Ruger's board.

How they plan to grow: New products and cost cuts while gun demand stays well below its pandemic peak. With a 25% owner that knows the industry, a full takeover later is possible, but nothing has been offered.

What to watch: Q3 results in early November and any next move by Beretta, such as board nominations or a full bid. Risk: the stock has lost the support of the $44.80 offer, and gun sales keep sliding.

4. Buckle (BKE) · ~$47, estimated value ~$47 (assumes no growth)

A debt-free mall clothing chain that one analyst follows, with insiders owning about 38%. The price now roughly matches its estimated value, so treat it as a steady dividend payer, not a bargain.

Latest news: September sales rose 2.2%, but sales at stores open over a year rose only 0.5%, slower than August. Year to date, sales are up 4.6%.

How they plan to grow: Slowly: denim and its own brands, a few new stores, and handing most of its cash to owners through regular and special dividends.

What to watch: October sales on Nov 5 and Q3 results in late November. Risk: teen fashion tastes shift, and there's little discount to protect you if they do.

5. Travelzoo (TZOO) · ~$5, estimated value ~$6 (assumes no growth)

A $51M travel-deals company whose founder's family owns about 36%. The stock is down about 59% since it switched to paid memberships, which cost money now but should bring steady fees later.

Latest news: Q2 revenue fell 3% and it lost $2.1M, which it blamed on world conflicts hurting travel. Membership renewals hit a record, and fees collected in advance rose to $13.4M from $8.7M at the start of the year.

How they plan to grow: Building recurring membership fees on top of its advertising business. Management expects revenue to grow again from Q3 onward.

What to watch: Q3 results, expected in late October: did revenue actually grow again? This is the highest-risk pick. It's tiny, it's losing money, it has only about $8M of cash, and the shareholders' equity on its books is below zero.

Also on the radar: Smith & Wesson (SWBI, sales up 32% last quarter and cheap even with no growth, but profits are still thin), and Shoe Station (SHOE, debt-free and down 36%, but profits roughly halved and an interim CEO is resetting its strategy). Left out on purpose: Sylvamo (SLVM) scores high, but its profits collapsed and demand for office paper is shrinking for good.
