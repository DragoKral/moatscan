The model judges value, not timing: these are great businesses priced well below what their cash earnings suggest. Nobody can reliably say which stocks rise in the next few weeks; these are the ones with the best odds over a year or more. Ranked by how temporary the problem looks.

1. PayPal (PYPL) · ~$56, estimated value ~$125

Still throws off a lot of cash and keeps buying back its own shares. The market is pricing it as if it will never grow again after a 2026 earnings miss, an outlook cut and a new CEO.

Worry: its checkout button is losing ground to Apple Pay and others. Watch whether that growth stabilizes under the new CEO.

2. Sprouts Farmers Market (SFM) · ~$64, estimated value ~$126

Healthy-food grocer that has grown about 19% a year with strong profits for a grocer. The stock is down about 19% this year even though recent earnings beat estimates.

Worry: lots of investors are betting against it, and the CEO has been selling shares. Next test: earnings on Oct 28.

3. EPAM Systems (EPAM) · ~$116, estimated value ~$230

Software engineering firm big companies hire to build their tech. Several insiders recently bought shares with their own money, one of the better signals in public filings.

Worry: AI tools may shrink demand for human programmers. Management says it isn't seeing that yet.

4. Lululemon (LULU) · ~$94, estimated value ~$309

A decade as one of retail's best businesses, now down about 46% this year: US sales falling, tariffs, product misses, no permanent CEO.

Biggest upside if the brand recovers, but the estimate assumes some of the old growth returns. If it's becoming just another athleisure brand, the real value is far lower.

5. Innoviva (INVA) · ~$21, estimated value ~$74

Collects royalties on GSK lung inhalers and sells a few hospital drugs (up 46% last quarter). Earns about 15 cents of cash a year per dollar of stock price.

Worry: royalties fade as those inhalers age, which the estimate doesn't fully capture. Real value is likely below $74.
