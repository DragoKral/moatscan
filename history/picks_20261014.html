These are great businesses priced well below what their cash earnings suggest. Nobody can reliably say which stocks rise in the next few weeks, so treat these as the best odds over a year or more, not promises. Ranked by how temporary the problem looks.

1. PayPal (PYPL) · ~$56, estimated value ~$125

PayPal still produces a lot of cash, and the stock is priced as if it will never grow again.

Latest news: In February, PayPal missed earnings, cut its 2026 outlook and replaced its CEO with Enrique Lores, the former HP chief, who started March 1. It also faces investor lawsuits over how it described checkout growth.

How they plan to grow: Lores is cutting at least $1.5B of costs over 2–3 years, about 40% of it through AI. He is shifting the checkout business from chasing volume to chasing profit, making Venmo earn more through added financial services, and getting merchants' catalogs ready for AI shopping assistants that buy on your behalf.

What to watch: Management says it will lay out a profit-growth plan beyond 2027 within about six months of June. Q3 earnings in late October/early November will show whether checkout is stabilizing.

2. Sprouts Farmers Market (SFM) · ~$64, estimated value ~$126

A healthy-food grocer that has grown about 19% a year with unusually strong profits for a grocer. The stock is down about 19% this year even though it keeps beating earnings estimates.

Latest news: Q2 sales were $2.3B with $129M profit. Full-year guidance is 5.5–6.5% sales growth, slower than its recent pace, which is why the stock cooled. Many investors are betting against it (about 15% of shares sold short), and the CEO sold some shares in October.

How they plan to grow: Opening 42 net new stores in 2026, its biggest year yet, and running its own produce distribution. Analysts project about 8% yearly revenue growth through 2029, to roughly $11B.

What to watch: Q3 earnings on Oct 28. Sales at stores open over a year are the number that matters.

3. EPAM Systems (EPAM) · ~$116, estimated value ~$230

Software engineers that big companies hire to build their tech. Several insiders recently bought shares with their own money.

Latest news: Q2 revenue grew only 4.5%, and 2026 guidance was cut again to 3–4% growth. US clients are barely growing, while Europe grew 11%.

How they plan to grow: Betting on AI instead of being replaced by it. Revenue from AI work is already over $160M a quarter (11% of sales) and has grown double digits six quarters in a row. Big multi-year AI contracts are expected to kick in during the first half of 2027. They're also overhauling US sales and buying back shares: about $1.6B returned to shareholders so far.

What to watch: Q3 earnings in early November: are the large AI deals getting signed, and is the US improving?

4. Lululemon (LULU) · ~$94, estimated value ~$309

A decade as one of retail's best businesses, now down about 46% this year.

Latest news: US sales have fallen five quarters in a row, tariffs cut profit margins, and some product launches flopped. Full-year revenue is guided flat and earnings down about 17%.

How they plan to grow: New CEO Heidi O'Neill, a 25-year Nike veteran who ran its product and brand, started Sept 8 with a mandate to fix product freshness in core categories. Growth is shifting abroad: China (still growing about 20%), Mexico (30+ stores planned), and six new countries in 2026. US store growth is being slowed.

What to watch: O'Neill's first strategy update and the December earnings report. The estimate assumes some of the old growth returns; if the brand keeps fading, real value is far lower.

5. Innoviva (INVA) · ~$21, estimated value ~$74

Collects royalties on GSK lung inhalers and sells hospital drugs. It earns about 15 cents of cash a year per dollar of stock price.

Latest news: Q2 revenue rose 19% to $120M, and hospital drug sales were up 46%. It showed an accounting loss from investments falling in value, not from the business itself.

How they plan to grow: Expects over $1B in royalties over the next five years, most of it from outside the US. It's using that cash to grow the hospital drug business (over $200M a year and growing 40–50%), launch zoliflodacin (a new pill for drug-resistant gonorrhea), and expand abroad through partners.

What to watch: The zoliflodacin launch and Q3 results in early November. Royalties fade as the inhalers age, which the estimate doesn't fully capture.
