This week's picks focus on small, overlooked companies: under $3B in market value, followed by few or no Wall Street analysts, beaten down from their highs, and still solidly profitable. The idea is to be contrarian where it's justified. Small stocks swing harder and can be thinly traded, so keep positions small and use limit orders. Nobody can reliably predict which stocks rise in the next few weeks; these are the best odds over a year or more, not promises.

1. Innoviva (INVA) · ~$21, estimated value ~$49, if no growth ~$27

A $1.5B royalty-and-drugs company that only five analysts follow. Even if it never grows, its cash earnings are worth more than today's price.

Latest news: Q2 revenue rose 19% to $120M, and hospital drug sales were up 46%. The reported loss came from its investment holdings dropping in value, not from the business.

How they plan to grow: Expects over $1B of royalties from GSK lung inhalers over five years and uses that cash to grow its hospital drugs (over $200M a year, growing 40–50%) and to launch zoliflodacin, a new pill for drug-resistant gonorrhea. Analysts expect about 9% revenue growth.

What to watch: Q3 results in early November and the zoliflodacin launch. Risk: royalties fade as the inhalers age.

2. Build-A-Bear Workshop (BBW) · ~$25, estimated value ~$34, if no growth ~$33

The classic contrarian setup: down about 65% from its high after a guidance cut, yet still expecting $60–68M of pre-tax profit this year on a $310M market value. Only three analysts cover it.

Latest news: In late August it cut its 2026 forecast to $500–525M in revenue. Q2 sales fell 7%, online sales fell 16%, and promotions and rent squeezed margins.

How they plan to grow: More stores and partner-run locations abroad (international franchise revenue up about 12%), wholesale deals that put its products in other retailers, and a new flagship at ICON Park in Orlando. It returned $23M to shareholders in the first half.

What to watch: Q3 results around early December, then the holiday season, which makes or breaks its year. Risk: kids' spending stays weak and online sales keep falling.

3. Buckle (BKE) · ~$45, estimated value ~$47 (assumes no growth)

A debt-free mall clothing chain that one analyst follows, with insiders owning about 38%. It isn't deeply discounted, but it's a steady cash machine that pays large dividends, and sales are quietly rising.

Latest news: August sales rose 3.6%, and sales at stores open over a year are up 3.2% so far this fiscal year. The stock is down about 24% from its high anyway.

How they plan to grow: Slow and steady: denim and private-label brands, a few new stores, and returning most of its cash to owners through regular and special dividends.

What to watch: Monthly sales reports, the first Thursday of each month (early November next), and Q3 results in late November. Risk: teen fashion turns, and the margin of safety is thin.

4. Sturm, Ruger (RGR) · ~$43, estimated value ~$55, if no growth ~$54

A US gun maker with no debt and a long profit record, followed by only two analysts. Insiders have been buying with their own money.

Latest news: This is a "special situation". Italian gunmaker Beretta launched a tender offer to buy up to about 2.4M shares (roughly 15% of the company) at $44.80, and there's a fight over board seats. The offer is set to expire Oct 15 unless extended.

How they plan to grow: New product launches and cost cuts while gun demand stays well below its pandemic peak. A big outside shareholder could push for a sale or a shake-up.

What to watch: Whether Beretta extends or raises its offer, the board-seat vote, and Q3 results in early November. Risk: gun sales keep sliding and the Beretta situation fizzles.

5. Travelzoo (TZOO) · ~$5, estimated value ~$7 (assumes no growth)

A $52M travel-deals company whose founder's family owns about 36%. It's down about 59% after switching to a paid-membership model, which hurts profits now in exchange for steady fees later.

Latest news: Q2 revenue fell 3% with a $2.1M loss, blamed on weaker travel demand and the cost of signing up paying members. Membership renewals hit a record, and it kept buying back shares.

How they plan to grow: Building recurring membership revenue on top of its advertising business. Management expects revenue to grow again starting in Q3.

What to watch: Q3 results in late October: did revenue actually return to growth? This is the highest-risk pick. It's tiny, it's currently losing money, and it only has about $8M of cash.

Also on the radar: Heritage Global (HGBL, a $44M asset-auction company where insiders are buying) and Shoe Station (SHOE, down 39% with one analyst covering it).
